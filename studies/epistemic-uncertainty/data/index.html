Epistemic vs. Physical Uncertainty Preferences

210 children and adolescents across urban Iran (N = 100), urban USA (N = 56), and hunter-horticulturalist Bolivian Tsimané communities with no prior exposure to dice (N = 54) chose between Roll-Then-Guess (epistemic) and Guess-Then-Roll (physical) dice games.

Epistemic vs. physical uncertainty choice by culture (Figure 3)

Share of participants choosing Roll-Then-Guess (epistemic uncertainty: die is rolled under an opaque cup before guessing) versus Guess-Then-Roll (physical uncertainty: participant guesses before the die is rolled). Dashed line marks 50% chance.

Stability of epistemic preference across age bands (Figure 2)

Across childhood and adolescence, participants consistently favored epistemic uncertainty with no developmental shift toward physical uncertainty—regardless of prior cultural familiarity with dice.
