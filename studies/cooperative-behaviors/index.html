Moral Development & Cooperation

The Emergence of Cooperative Behaviors, Norms, and Strategies Across Five Diverse Societies

Dorsa Amir

Ages 5–13 and 18+

OSF repository Preprint

Research questions

  • How do cooperative behaviors—fairness, trustworthiness, forgiveness, and honesty—vary across diverse societies in childhood?
  • How do peer and adult norms shape the development of children’s cooperative decisions?
  • What distinct cooperative strategies emerge across development and cultures?

This cross-cultural investigation examines a suite of four cooperative behaviors in children ages 5 to 13 (N = 413) alongside normative judgments from peers (N = 163) and adults (N = 86) across five diverse societies: urban communities in the United States, rural communities in Uganda, Canada, and Peru, and hunter-horticulturalist Shuar communities in Amazonian Ecuador.

Across sites, children’s cooperative behaviors and judgments vary substantially across cultures and increasingly converge toward community-specific norms in middle childhood, revealing three distinct strategies—maximization, generic cooperation, and partner-contingent cooperation—that shift across age and society.
