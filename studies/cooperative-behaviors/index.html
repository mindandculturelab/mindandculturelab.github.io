Moral Development & Cooperation

The Emergence of Cooperative Behaviors, Norms, and Strategies Across Five Diverse Societies

Dorsa Amir, Richard E. Ahl, Matthew R. Jordan, Hannah Bolotin, Michael Bogese, Gabriela T. González, Tara Callaghan, Lawrence S. Sugiyama, Joshua Kabatabazi, Emily Otali, Patrick Tusiime, Samantha Bangayan, J. Josh Snodgrass, & Katherine McAuliffe

Science Advances (2025) · Ages 5–13 & 18+

Look at the data Experience the procedure Paper (PDF)

Research questions

  • How do fairness, trustworthiness, forgiveness, and honesty develop across childhood in culturally and ecologically diverse communities?
  • When do children's cooperative behaviors diverge across societies, and how closely do they align with local peer and adult norms?
  • Do children approach fairness, trust, forgiveness, and honesty as unified or distinct cooperative strategies?

Human societies rely on multiple forms of cooperation—sharing resources fairly, honoring trust, forgiving accidental transgressions, and acting honestly even when unobserved—yet most developmental studies examine a single prosocial task in a single Western population. In this pre-registered cross-cultural investigation, 413 children aged 5 to 13 (mean age 8.78) completed an identical four-task behavioral battery across five diverse communities: hunter-horticulturalist Shuar communities in Amazonian Ecuador (n = 67), rural San Pedro de Saño in Peru (n = 86), Tooro communities in Fort Portal, Uganda (n = 108), Antigonish, Canada (n = 71), and Boston, USA (n = 81).

Each child completed four hands-on tasks (Figure 1 in the paper): (A) the Inequity Game measuring fairness via green (accept) and red (reject) handles over advantageous 4–1 and 4–0 candy allocations; (B) the Trust Apparatus measuring trustworthiness when a partner forfeits a short tool piece so the actor can assemble a long hooked rod to retrieve a 6-candy dish; (C) the Forgiveness Task measuring how many of 5 scratch-art sheets children return to a peer who accidentally ruined their drawing; and (D) an unobserved Honesty Task measuring whether children return 3 extra candies from a box supposed to contain only 1. Alongside the behavioral battery, 163 peer-aged children and 86 adults across the communities reported normative expectations across matching vignettes.

Across societies, cooperative behaviors showed distinct developmental trajectories and converged toward community-specific peer and adult norms during middle childhood. Factor and latent profile analyses revealed three overarching behavioral strategies across development: payoff maximization, generic unconditional cooperation, and partner-contingent cooperation.
