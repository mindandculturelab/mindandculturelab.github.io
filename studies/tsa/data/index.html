Cooperation · Rivero, Vasil, & Tomasello (2026)

Coordination, Strategy & Communication

Forty same-aged dyads and triads (100 children, ages 2.0–4.0) completed three color test trials and one animal generalization trial. Filter the sample to inspect success, strategy, and peer communication.

Test trials (Trials 1–3): matching colors

Share of the three red/blue test trials in which partners placed the same color magnet on the box within 70 seconds (Table 1 in the paper). By 3.17 years of age, triads succeeded at rates indistinguishable from dyads.

Domain generalization (Trial 4): matching animals

Without new instruction, red and blue magnets were swapped for pig and cow magnets (Table 2 in the paper). When groups could no longer repeat a previously rewarded color, 2-year-old triads dropped to 1 of 10 while every other condition reached 10 of 10.

How groups succeeded: collaborative vs. individualistic strategies

Across all four trials, successful openings were coded as collaborative (checking a partner’s side or communicating about a specific key) or individualistic (chance, trial and error, repeating one color, or placing a partner’s key for them; Table 3 in the paper).
