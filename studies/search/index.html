Study · Exploration & Innovation

Goals shape children's explore-exploit tendencies

Alissa Rivero, Benjamin Pitt, & Dorsa Amir · Ages 4–9 · Volcano Escape Game

3D procedure Task code (GitHub) ↗ Related topic

Research questions

  • When children search a spatially structured rock map, when do they explore new tiles versus exploit known ones?
  • Do goal framings—Volcano Escape versus Bigger Reward—reshape those explore–exploit tendencies?

Children play the tablet Volcano Escape Game from SEARCH-App: an 8×8 map of 64 squares with spatially correlated rewards. Each click reveals a rock whose size tracks the square's point value; nearby squares tend to share similar payoffs. One square starts revealed, and each map allows 25 clicks. On every click, a child can open a covered square to learn its rock (explore) or re-click a revealed square to collect another rock of about the same size (exploit).

Holding that map fixed, SEARCH-App counterbalances two framings: Volcano Escape (collected rocks build a path away from the volcano—“how far you'll get away”) and Bigger Reward (rocks stack toward a sized prize—“what sized prize you'll get”), testing whether motivation, not just search skill, drives developmental explore–exploit patterns.

Task implementation: github.com/alissarivero/SEARCH-App
