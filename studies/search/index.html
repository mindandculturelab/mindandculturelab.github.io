Goals Shape Children's Explore-Exploit Tendencies

Alissa Rivero · Ages 4–9 · Grid Search Task

3D procedure Task code (GitHub) ↗ Related topic

Research questions

  • When children search a spatially structured board for rewards, when do they explore new tiles versus exploit known ones?
  • Do instructed goals—maximize points versus learn the board—reshape those explore–exploit tendencies?

Children play a tablet Grid Search Task: an 8×8 board of 64 squares with spatially correlated point values (nearby tiles tend to share similar rewards; darker colors mark higher payoffs). One square starts revealed, and each board allows 25 clicks. On every click, a child can open a covered square to learn its value (explore) or re-click a revealed square to collect points again (exploit).

Holding that apparatus fixed, the study varies the goal children are given—collect as many points as possible, or learn how the board works—testing whether motivation, not just search skill, drives developmental explore–exploit patterns.

Task implementation: github.com/alissarivero/SEARCH (Cordova GridSearch app adapted for this developmental paradigm).
