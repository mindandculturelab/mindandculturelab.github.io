Moral Development & Cooperation

Children Are More Forgiving of Accidental Harms Across Development

Dorsa Amir, Richard E. Ahl, Winter S. Parsons, & Katherine McAuliffe

Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (2021) · Ages 5–10

Look at the data Experience the procedure Paper (PDF)

Research questions

  • How do intent (accidental vs. intentional harm) and remorse (apologetic vs. unremorseful) shape children's behavioral forgiveness across ages 5 to 10?
  • Do children forgive peers at above-chance rates even without explicit apologies?
  • How do children's verbal moral evaluations of a transgressor relate to their costly behavioral forgiveness?

Repairing social relationships after a harm requires weighing what happened against what a partner intended and whether they feel remorse. While classic moral judgment studies show that children increasingly privilege mental states over outcomes in verbal ratings, far less is known about how intent and remorse guide children's actual behavioral forgiveness when they can impose a non-selfish cost on a transgressor.

Two hundred fifty-seven children aged 5 to 10 were randomly assigned across five between-subjects conditions: Baseline (no harm, n = 55), Accidental + Remorse (n = 53), Accidental + No Remorse (n = 50), Intentional + Remorse (n = 49), and Intentional + No Remorse (n = 50). After learning that another child had ruined their rainbow scratch-art drawing, participants were given five of the other child's drawings and left in privacy (turning the experimenter away) with an envelope to return sheets to the peer, a trash can to throw sheets away (with no option to keep any for themselves), and a desk bell to ring when finished, followed by a 6-point moral evaluation scale.

Across conditions, children showed remarkably high forgiveness—returning an average of 3.47 of 5 sheets (well above chance, p < .0001; Baseline M = 3.76). Crucially, a significant Age × Intent interaction revealed that across ages 5 to 10, older children became substantially more forgiving of accidental harms relative to intentional harms—mirroring the developmental sharpening of mental-state reasoning in real interpersonal repair.

Citation: Amir, D., Ahl, R. E., Parsons, W. S., & McAuliffe, K. (2021). Children are more forgiving of accidental harms across development. Journal of Experimental Child Psychology, 205, 105081. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2020.105081
