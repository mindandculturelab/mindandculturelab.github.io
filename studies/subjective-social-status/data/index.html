Social Cognition · Amir, Valeggia, Srinivasan, Sugiyama, & Dunham (2019)

MacArthur Ladder & Social Comparison Index

363 children and adolescents (ages 4–18) across Vadodara (India), New Haven (USA), Gran Chaco Toba/Qom (Argentina), and Upano Valley & Cross-Cutucú Shuar (Ecuador) placed their families on the 10-rung MacArthur Ladder (1 = top, 10 = bottom, reverse-scored here so 10 = highest status) and answered 4 concrete social comparison questions.

Perceived family standing across societies and age bands

In the raw worksheet (Figure 1), rung 1 is the top of the ladder (most money, schooling, jobs, respect) and rung 10 is the bottom; values below are oriented so 10 = top rung and 1 = bottom rung for intuitive reading.

Alignment between concrete Social Comparison Index (SCI) and Ladder standing

Across societies (except remote Cross-Cutucú), children reporting relatively nicer houses, more food, more money, and more things (lower raw SCI = higher material standing) placed their families higher on the MacArthur Ladder.

Participant-level MacArthur Ladder & SCI dataset

Each row represents one child/adolescent in the cross-cultural sample (N = 363; raw worksheet rung 1 = top to 10 = bottom; SCI 4 = more than most on all 4 items, 12 = less than most). One-week (N = 43) and one-year (N = 665) India test–retest CSVs are available on the study page.
